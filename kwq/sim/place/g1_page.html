P6g gate G1: which BULK placement goes into the kernel

The simulator, checked against lab, compares the accepted gossip scheme with today's kwq and the alternatives on every scenario of the plan. The gossip scheme meets the criteria least often; P8, a sharded idle mask with crest's donor flag, meets them most often at a fraction of today's line traffic. The decision is crest's.

A. P8 into the kernel (recommended)

Keeps today's placement and replaces what costs: the steal scan by a donor line, the one idle line by 16-CPU shards, plus a far probe when the neighbourhood is full and a tick that rescues a starved CPU. Best of all policies on mean wait (A1f: 737 of 925 runs, today 612), on the tails (W1a: 1333 of 1370, today 1165) and on movement (W5); over the 100-seed worst cases every run meets W5 and 97 % meet W1a (today 86 %). Behind today on idle-while-waiting (W2a: 692 against 810), on recovery after a step (W2b: 199 against 226) and on herding against the oracle (W4o: 95 % against 99.8 %).

B. The gossip scheme as planned

Cheapest in lines and strongest where load must cross L3s and packages and in overload recovery, through its hand-off. Its tables are milliseconds old and four entries small: an island on a flat 128-CPU machine waits two orders of magnitude longer than with an idle mask, and its average waits are the worst of all policies.

C. Revise first

A hybrid: P8 within a package, the gossip hand-off across packages, which only the synthetic two-socket model (unvalidated) needs today. More simulation before the kernel.

Decisions for crest

  1. Which policy goes to P6g.2: A, B or C.
  2. Criterion A1 as written (1.2 x the oracle's mean wait) fails every policy at waits of a few microseconds, where the oracle sits below 1 us. Adopt A1f, max(1.2 x the oracle, the oracle + 10 us), in its place?
  3. W5 (no item moved more than three times) fails every stealing policy in a flash crowd, where halving spreads work in log2 steps. Keep it for steady loads only?
  4. The model's two known gaps, below under Model check: steals succeed five times less often than on lab, and the share limit's cost to large items is not modelled. Accept them for G1?
  5. W4 (at most 8 sources onto one core within 50 us) fails the oracle itself in 39 % of the main runs: it counts an idle core taking items one after another, not only pile-ups. Judge herding as W4o, against the oracle's own herding, and count pile-ups (items landing behind another source's queued item) in P6g.3's kernel measurements?
  6. W2b counts a run as never recovered when the policy's steady state misses W2a's 2 %, so it repeats W2a there. Keep it as written?
  7. P8's cascade wake (a thief that leaves its donor at 0.5 ms or more wakes one more) changes no run by more than 4 % in the ablations. Leave it out of the kernel?

What A means in the kernel (P6g.2)

stepwhat
K1kwq_bulk_idle, one cpuset today, cut into one line per 16 CPUs in topology order, each written by its own CPUs; the group size a loader tunable, so the 4-CPU guest can run groups of 1 and exercise the far probe
K2the donor bitmap and a per-CPU hint naming the longest stealable list, written by its owner at decision points
K3the idle path: kwq_steal()'s scan of every list in reach replaced by the donor steal; steal wakes as today, the cascade, the pull at the end of a pass, the bound, the lone item
K4kwq_pick_any() reads the group lines, then one far group from a per-CPU cursor; it avoids donors; spill for fixed-CPU queues
K5the starved tick: a per-CPU callout(9), armed while the CPU holds stealable work; it runs in softclock, so it covers user real-time and timeshare hogs, not interrupt storms
K6kern.kwq.bulk.place: 0 today's code, 1 P8, flippable under load; ATF cases for each step on the guest (GENERIC-DEBUG), then lab and, with your permission, a07
